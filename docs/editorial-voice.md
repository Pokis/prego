# Editorial voice

## Reader promise

Speak to an intelligent adult who may be tired, frightened, excited, unsure or reading quickly on a phone. Never assume this pregnancy was planned, that the reader has a partner, or that a particular birth or feeding method defines success.

## Style

- Give the direct answer first.
- Prefer short sentences and concrete verbs.
- Explain a medical term in the sentence where it first appears.
- Say when timing or recommendations vary.
- Use “common” without implying “must be tolerated.”
- Use “contact your care team” only with a reason or threshold.
- Keep urgency unambiguous and action-oriented.

Avoid cute body commentary, weight shame, competitive milestones, fear-based framing, stereotypes about mothers or fathers, and moral language around feeding, birth or medicine.

Target approximately grade 7–8 English. Readability is not simplification of risk; it is reduction of unnecessary complexity.
