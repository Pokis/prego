# Sources and clinical review

## Source priority

1. WHO for the global foundation.
2. National health authorities and statutory guidance.
3. National clinical guidelines.
4. Established professional clinical bodies.

Commercial guides and community stories may reveal reader questions but never support a medical claim.

## Review cadence

- `rapid-review`: vaccination, medicine, emergency services, public services and statutory schedules; maximum 90 days.
- `annual`: most health guidance and regional pathways; maximum one year.
- `stable`: foundational guidance that changes less often, still checked when upstream guidance changes.

## Status workflow

`draft` → `editorial-ready` → `clinical-approved`.

Any expired review or materially changed source becomes `needs-review`. Clinical approval identifies the reviewer, qualification, approval date and expiry. Approval attaches to exact content, not a topic label.

The current generated content is intentionally `editorial-ready`. That status may pass the technical publication gate but does not imply clinical approval. The live product must retain its pending-review presentation and `noindex` metadata until qualified review of the exact copy is recorded.
